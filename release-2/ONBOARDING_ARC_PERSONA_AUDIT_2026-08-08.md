# Persona audit — the 60-second "Outcome Orchestration" onboarding arc

**Scope:** the journey segment from the *analyzing* beat → the paced pillar/integrity education (pre-activation), which the proposed narrated script would replace/refine. **Milestone:** activation (the first real decision). **Method note (honesty):** this is a **design-time** simulation of a *not-yet-built* arc — no live screenshots or funnel-state dumps, because the screen doesn't exist yet. It's run through the persona engine (role/experience/industry/methodology/attitude) against the script and the current flow. Baseline build verified green (`_S10` 66/66). Personas per your ask: **scrum PM**, **non-technical owner**, **skeptical veteran PM**.

## The constraint that reframes everything
The fast pass takes **up to 60 seconds regardless**. So the real question is **not** "narrated arc vs. instant access" — instant access doesn't exist. It's "**narrated arc vs. a 60-second spinner**." That changes the verdict on "skip": a user can't skip the *wait*, only the *narration*. So "skip" must route to **something**, and the best something is *their own plan being analyzed live* — not a progress bar. Hold that; it resolves the hardest persona.

## Opening stances (in voice)

**Maya — scrum PM** (delivery PM · seasoned · software · scrum · AI-eager): *"Our 'plan' is a backlog and a goals one-pager, not a document. I'll give you 60 seconds if you respect my time and tell me something real. Lecture me about 'confidence bands,' or show me a Gantt chart and call it my plan, and I'm gone."*

**The non-technical owner** (business/functional owner · delegates detail · limited PM vocabulary · neutral): *"I own this outcome; my team lives in the plan. I want to know if it's on track and what needs **me**. I don't know 'feasibility as a dimension.' Ask me to confirm granular details my team owns and I'll feel this isn't built for me."*

**The skeptical veteran PM** (seasoned · AI-skeptical · wants signal fast · resents hand-holding): *"I've run 50 of these. Don't explain my job to me. Show me a real risk in **my** plan I don't already see — fast — or I skip. And don't sell me a Framework."*

### What the walk shows

**Maya (scrum PM) — largely works, with two watch-items.** "Living hypothesis" (Scene 2) and the identity shift (Scene 3) are agile-native and land. Two risks: (a) Scene 2's visualization must **not** look like a static waterfall doc/Gantt or she rejects the core metaphor on sight; (b) Scene 4 is where a seasoned PM's patience thins — it's the "mechanism" stretch, ironically the very thing you're trying to de-emphasize. "Outcome Orchestration" (Scene 5) risks a mild framework-fatigue eye-roll. She won't bounce if it's personalized and skippable, and it gives her something her standup doesn't: a reframed role.

**Non-technical owner — the biggest winner.** This arc directly fixes her current failure mode. The live build's weakness for her is that it's *plan-mechanic-first* and asks the *wrong person to confirm granular details* (a known journey-audit finding). The arc leads with **outcome** (her language), casts her as the **leader** (her actual role), and — critically — teases the first act as **owning the outcome**, an owner-appropriate decision. Two conditions gate the win: Scene 4 must stay **plain questions** ("is it clear? is the evidence visible? can you catch drift?"), not pillar jargon (Viability/Grounding/Adaptability terminology + "inference chains" will glaze her); and Scene 6's first decision must be **outcome-level, not "confirm the Wi-Fi assumption."** If it dumps her into a granular confirm at second 59, the whole good frame collapses at the finish line.

**Skeptical veteran — the arc's risk, and the reason the "skip" design matters.** The aspirational identity framing that wins the owner reads as **condescension** to him ("don't explain my job"), and framework-naming (Scene 5) confirms a "they're selling me" suspicion. He's the most likely to reach for skip around **Scene 3 (~25–30s)**. But — the fast-pass reframe rescues this: he can't skip the wait, so if "skip" drops him into **his plan being analyzed live, with real findings starting to surface**, that's *exactly his speed* (evidence, not philosophy), and skeptics convert on evidence. Without that route — if "skip" just yields a spinner or, worse, there's no skip — the arc **lowers** activation for this segment.

## Universal vs. persona-specific friction

**Universal wins**
- Leading with **why** (outcome + "you lead the outcome") fixes the diagnosed gap for all three — it's the framing the current mechanism-first flow lacks.
- Filling the unavoidable 60s with **meaning** beats a spinner for everyone.

**Universal risk**
- **Scene 4 is the relapse point.** Defining three pillars is the "what/how" you're trying to spend *less* time on. Keep it short, plain-question, and shown (not narrated jargon), or you've reintroduced the mechanism-heaviness under new art.

**Persona-specific**
- Identity-shift + framework-naming: **helps the owner, fine for Maya, actively risks the veteran.** Don't let the philosophy run long or preachy.
- Agile-native (Maya): Scene 2's visual must not read as a static document/Gantt.
- Framework fatigue: "Outcome Orchestration" needs a light, earned touch — name it once, don't dwell.

---

## Segments: activated better vs. at risk
- **Activated better:** the non-technical owner (largest gain — the arc repairs her wrong-person / plan-first problem) and the why-seeking / AI-eager PM.
- **At risk of loss:** the skeptical veteran and the impatient "just let me in" user — **unless** skip routes to live processing of their real plan and the arc is personalized.

## The single change that most widens the funnel
**Personalize the arc with the ingested plan, and make "skip" a route to *their plan being analyzed live* — not a spinner and not a teleport.** Then the philosophy is the *default* cover for the unavoidable 60s wait (winning the owner and the why-seeker), while the skeptic/impatient can watch the machine work on *their* real plan and catch a finding — both paths converging on the **same first decision** when processing completes.

## Recommendations, ranked by funnel impact
1. **Two modes over the same 60s, one destination.** Default = the narrated model-onboarding; "Skip the intro →" = live analysis of *their* plan (findings surfacing). Both end at the first decision. This is the veteran's lifeline and harmless to everyone else.
2. **Personalize from second 1.** Scene 1's outcome is *their* outcome; Scene 2 builds *their* plan; the assumptions/inferences that appear are *their* real gaps. Makes Scene 6's "dissolve into your project" **literal** and keeps it honest (not a generic reel).
3. **Guarantee the first decision is owner-appropriate** (define/own the outcome), not a granular confirm — or the owner win evaporates at Scene 6.
4. **Cut Scene 4 to plain questions, kept short.** Three questions shown, not three pillars lectured. Protects the veteran's patience and the owner's comprehension.
5. **Fire-once + returning-user fast path.** Second project shouldn't re-watch 60s of philosophy; but since fast-pass still runs, default returning users to the live-processing view.
6. **Keep it advisory, never overpromising.** "Surface what matters, strengthen your judgment, help you steer" is honest; do not let art imply "OSLO achieves your outcome." (No dark patterns — hard constraint.)
7. **Canon check:** run "Outcome Orchestration" and "living hypothesis" against `CANONICAL_GLOSSARY` before the narration is locked, to avoid term drift.

## Bottom line
The arc is the right medicine for the diagnosed problem (too much *what*, not enough *why*), and it's strongest for the persona you currently serve worst — the non-technical owner. It's neutral-to-positive for the scrum PM. Its one real hazard is the skeptical veteran, and the fast-pass constraint hands you the fix: because nobody can skip the wait, "skip" becomes "show me my plan working," which is exactly what the skeptic wanted. Build the two-mode, personalized version — not a generic sizzle reel — and it widens the top of the funnel instead of narrowing it for the skeptics.
