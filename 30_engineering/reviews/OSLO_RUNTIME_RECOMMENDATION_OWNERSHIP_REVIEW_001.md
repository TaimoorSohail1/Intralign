## 0. The Decisive Doctrinal Test

OSLO's documented **core principle** (`OSLO_ARCHITECTURE_BASELINE_V1.md` §2): *"strict separation of concerns. Each layer has a single responsibility."* And §1 frames the layers as separating *"what is known, what is implied, what is judged, **what is allowed**, and what is explained."* **Governance = "what is allowed" (authority).** Recommendation generation = an **advisory cognitive act** ("what could we consider doing") — *implication/judgment about response*, not authority.

This test governs the whole review: **generation is cognition; Governance is authority.** Any model that makes **Governance *generate*** recommendations violates Governance's documented non-responsibility ("no recommendation generation") **and** the separation-of-concerns doctrine. Conversely, any model where recommendations are generated **without** Governance constraint contradicts the documented Stage 11→12 sequence ("recommendations … anchored to Issues and **constrained by Governance policy**"). The faithful answer must satisfy **both**: *cognition generates; Governance governs.*

## 1. Architectural Assessment

### Option A — New Recommendation Layer (cognition tier)
- **Honors separation of concerns:** generation is a distinct cognitive responsibility, separate from Governance authority. ✅
- **Resolves the ownership gap** with clear first-class ownership. ✅
- **Flaw as drawn:** the linear `Judgment → Recommendation → Governance` places generation **entirely before** Governance, contradicting documented **Stage 11 (Issue → Governance disposition) → Stage 12 (Recommendation, constrained by Governance)**. ❌ (fixable by placement/constraint)
- **Cost:** a 7th top-level layer = **new doctrine** (owner-ratifiable); risk of layer proliferation against the under-review architecture representation (GOV-ARCH-001).

### Option B — Governance-Owned Recommendation Engine
- **Aligns with the documented Stage 11→12 sequence** (recommendations after Issue disposition). ✅
- **Preserves the 6-layer count** (minimal structural change). ✅
- **Decisive flaw:** it makes **Governance *own and generate*** recommendations — **directly violating Governance's documented non-responsibility** ("Governance … does not generate recommendations") **and** the separation-of-concerns doctrine (authority must not also be cognition). The prompt lists "may blur governance and advisory generation" as a *risk*; on the evidence it is not a risk but a **doctrinal contradiction**. ❌❌
- **Consequence:** Option B trades one documented violation (no layer owns generation) for another (Governance now generates) — it does not actually restore doctrinal consistency; it relocates the breach into the authority layer, the worst place for it.

**Assessment:** Option A is **doctrinally sounder** (generation stays in cognition) but **mis-placed** as drawn; Option B is **flow-correct** but **doctrinally wrong** (Governance generating). Neither, as literally stated, is ratifiable. The faithful resolution combines A's *ownership* (cognition generates) with B's *sequencing* (governed, after disposition).

## 2. Recommendation Ownership Decision

**Recommend a Modified Option A: recommendations are a *governed cognitive output*.**
- **Generated by cognition** — a **Recommendation responsibility in the cognition tier**, after Judgment (preserves "Governance does not generate").
- **Governed by Governance** — generation is **constrained by** Governance policy (posture/tier), and the resulting recommendations are **exposed/suppressed/deferred/blocked** by Governance exactly as Issues are (preserves Stage 11→12 and Governance's authority role).
- **Open owner choice:** whether this cognition responsibility is elevated to a **named 7th Layer** (full Option A) or expressed as a **cognitive service/engine** owned by the cognition tier (not by Governance). Either satisfies the doctrine; the choice is granularity, not principle.
- **Reject Option B as stated** (Governance owning generation) — it violates a documented non-responsibility and the separation doctrine.

### Answers to Review Questions
- **Q1 — Which option preserves original intent?** **Neither as drawn; the Modified-A model does.** Original intent (separation of concerns; Governance = "what is allowed") requires **cognition to generate** and **Governance to govern**. Option A keeps generation in cognition (right) but mis-orders it (fixable); Option B puts generation in Governance (wrong — violates non-responsibility). Modified Option A is the faithful synthesis.
- **Q2 — Is a recommendation a cognitive layer / service / governed output / other?** **A *governed cognitive output*.** It is produced by a cognitive responsibility (generative) and is **constrained and exposed by Governance** (governed). It is **not** a pure independent reasoning artifact (it is constrained by Governance, Stage 12) and **not** a Governance function (Governance disclaims generation). Whether that cognitive responsibility is a **Layer or a service** is the owner's granularity decision.
- **Q3 — Where are Clarification Requests generated?** **A separate cognitive construct, sibling to recommendation generation — not conflated with it.** Both are generative cognitive outputs, but they serve **different purposes and consumers**: a **Recommendation** proposes *action* (→ user action → execution/reanalysis); a **Clarification Request** seeks *information to improve understanding* (→ user input → Knowledge/Confidence → reanalysis). They may **co-locate** in the cognition tier (a generative-advisory family) but remain **distinct objects** with distinct downstream flows. (Note: Clarification Engine is documented **Planned/unowned** — assigning it is a proposed extension requiring ratification.)
- **Q4 — Does `Issue → Governance → Recommendation` mean recommendations are fundamentally *governed* artifacts rather than independent reasoning artifacts?** **They are *governed cognitive* artifacts — both, not either/or.** The documented sequence proves they are **not independent** (they are constrained by Governance, generated after Issue disposition). But Governance's documented non-responsibility proves they are **not Governance-generated** either. The precise reading: recommendations are **cognitively generated outputs that are governed** — generation belongs to cognition; constraint and exposure belong to Governance. "Governed" describes their **authorization status**, not their **producer**.
- **Q5 — Redesign preserving the six layers + Release 1?** Preserve all six layers unchanged; add **recommendation generation as a governed cognitive responsibility** positioned **after Judgment and after Governance disposition of Issues**, constrained by Governance and exposed by Governance; treat **Clarification** as a sibling cognitive construct (distinct object); treat **MRI** as a **Communication-layer diagnostic** (renders governed Reasoning/Judgment outputs, not a cognitive layer); treat **UX panels** as **presentation constructs** over runtime objects. **Ownership model:** Reasoning→Findings; Judgment→Issues/severity/confidence/CAF; **Cognition (Recommendation responsibility)→Recommendations + Clarification Requests**, constrained by Governance; Governance→exposure/authorization (incl. of recommendations); Communication→render (incl. MRI). **Migration impact:** additive (a new cognitive responsibility + governed flow); no existing layer loses a documented responsibility; one **doctrinal addition** (the recommendation cognition responsibility) requiring owner ratification; reconcile with Stage 11→12 and the GOV-ARCH-001 review.

## 4. Architecture Evolution Recommendation

OSLO **should evolve minimally and additively**, consistent with documented intent:
- **Add a recommendation-generation responsibility in the cognition tier** (Layer *or* service — owner's granularity call), explicitly **governed by** the existing Governance Layer. This is the **only** doctrinally-consistent way to resolve the gap (cognition generates; Governance governs).
- **Do not** relocate generation into Governance (Option B) — it violates documented non-responsibility and separation of concerns.
- **Sequence the change after** the **GOV-ARCH-001/001A/000** architecture-representation review (the representation is under governance review; binding a layer/service now risks rework).
- **Keep the first increment minimal:** recommendation production first; Clarification and alternative-resolution-paths as **explicit, separate** owner decisions (avoid bundling multiple Planned/undocumented responsibilities at once).
- **Confirm the well-supported reframings** (MRI as Communication diagnostic; panels as presentation constructs) — these require no new cognition and resolve real gaps.

**Justification:** this path resolves the documented gap, preserves the six documented layers and their single-responsibility doctrine, honors the documented Stage 11→12 governed sequence, and changes the architecture by the **smallest doctrinally-coherent addition** — a governed cognitive recommendation responsibility — rather than by overloading the authority layer.

## 5. Final Verdict

**RATIFY MODIFIED OPTION A** *(recommendations as a **governed cognitive output**: cognition generates, Governance governs)* — **as a recommendation requiring owner ratification.**

**Conditions (modifications):**
- **M-1:** Generation is a **cognition-tier responsibility**, **not** Governance-owned (reject Option B's Governance-generates framing — it violates the documented Governance non-responsibility and separation doctrine).
- **M-2:** Placement honors documented **Stage 11→12**: Issues disposed by Governance → recommendations generated **constrained by** Governance → Governance **governs exposure** of recommendations → Communication renders. (Fixes Option A's mis-ordering.)
- **M-3:** Owner decides **Layer vs. cognitive service** granularity (both satisfy the doctrine).
- **M-4:** **Clarification** and **alternative-resolution-paths** are **separate, explicit** owner decisions (Planned/undocumented today), not auto-bundled.
- **M-5:** Sequence after **GOV-ARCH-001/001A/000**; confirm **MRI-as-Communication-diagnostic** and **panels-as-presentation-constructs** (well-supported).

**Why not Ratify Option A (as drawn):** mis-orders generation before Governance, contradicting Stage 11→12. **Why not Ratify Option B:** Governance generating recommendations **violates** a documented non-responsibility and the separation-of-concerns doctrine — it relocates the breach into the authority layer. **Why not Reject Both:** the gap is real and resolvable by the smallest doctrinally-coherent change (Modified Option A); rejection leaves recommendation production unowned.
