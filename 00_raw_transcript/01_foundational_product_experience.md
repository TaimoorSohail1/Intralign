# OSLO UI Raw Transcript — Part 1
## Foundational Product Experience and Workspace Model

## Q1 — First-Time User Emotional Outcome
**Question:** When a brand-new user finishes the first 60-second OSLO experience, what is the single most important feeling or realization?

**Recommendation:** “Now I understand my project.”

**Rationale:** OSLO’s first job is orientation, not plan generation. Trust, action, and control emerge after understanding.

---

## Q2 — Primary Object Users Open
**Question:** What is the main object users think they are opening?

**Recommendation:** Outcome Space.

**Rationale:** “Project” pulls users back into legacy PM thinking. Outcome Space better supports intended outcomes, ambiguity, assumptions, confidence, execution signals, and governance state.

---

## Q3 — First View Inside Outcome Space
**Question:** When users enter an Outcome Space, what should they see first?

**Recommendation:** What OSLO Understands, represented as artifact-derived intelligence.

**Key refinement:** Artifacts can represent what OSLO understands, but should not become the primary product object. Artifacts are evidence containers supporting OSLO’s synthesized understanding.

---

## Q4 — Proactive vs Reactive Behavior
**Question:** Should OSLO primarily behave proactively or reactively?

**Recommendation:** Primarily proactive, with reactive exploration as a secondary capability.

**Rationale:** Users should not have to remember to ask whether ownership is ambiguous, confidence dropped, assumptions drifted, or stakeholders became misaligned.

---

## Q5 — Chat Visibility
**Question:** How visible should chat be?

**Recommendation:** Side companion plus context-triggered expansion.

**Rationale:** Central chat would teach the wrong mental model. OSLO should not feel like another AI chat wrapper.

---

## Q6 — Main User Activity
**Question:** What should users spend most of their time doing?

**Recommendation:** Interacting with and improving artifacts while OSLO continuously optimizes outcome quality.

**Rationale:** Users do not merely observe intelligence. They want to improve reality.

---

## Q7 — Navigation Basis
**Question:** Should navigation organize around artifacts, intelligence, or hybrid?

**Recommendation:** Artifact-native hybrid with embedded intelligence.

**Rationale:** Artifacts are where users work. Intelligence is the layer that lives on top of everything.

---

## Q8 — Permanently Visible Information
**Question:** What information should remain permanently visible?

**Decision:** Outcome Confidence, Clarity, Alignment, Feasibility, Attention Queue.

**Rationale:** These become OSLO’s persistent outcome-integrity health bar.

---

## Q9 — Role-Specific Interfaces
**Question:** Should different users experience entirely different interfaces?

**Recommendation:** Role-based workspaces with adaptive intelligence.

**Rationale:** Same truth, different lens. PMs, executives, governance users, and teams should see role-relevant views without fragmenting reality.

---

## Q10 — Interaction Model
**Question:** What interaction model should OSLO feel like?

**Recommendation:** Hybrid: Command Center + Split Pane + Layered Intelligence.

**Rationale:** OSLO must support understanding, editing artifacts, reviewing recommendations, inspecting evidence, and interacting with OSLO.

---

## Q11 — Explainability Depth
**Question:** How explainable should OSLO be when intelligence changes?

**Recommendation:** Score + evidence + source lineage + reasoning path.

**Rationale:** Explainability is a core interface primitive, not a debugging feature.

---

## Q12 — OSLO’s “Inbox Zero”
**Question:** What is OSLO’s equivalent of Inbox Zero?

**Recommendation:** Outcome trajectory is trustworthy.

**Refinement:** Healthy state should include Clarity, Alignment, Feasibility, no intervention-level risks, and stable or improving confidence.

---

## Q13 — Time vs Structure Navigation
**Question:** Should users navigate by time or structure?

**Recommendation:** Structure-first with time as a secondary lens.

**Rationale:** Users understand complex outcomes structurally and inspect change over time.

---

## Q14 — Product Analogy
**Question:** What should OSLO feel most like?

**Recommendation:** Cursor-like / AI-native IDE for outcome systems, with mission-control and governance layers added.

**Rationale:** Cursor layers AI into familiar work surfaces without replacing them.

---

## Q15 — Sentence Completion
**Prompt:** OSLO should feel like ______ for outcome intelligence.

**Emerging answer:** Cursor for Outcome Orchestration / AI-native IDE for outcome systems.

---

## Q16 — Intelligence Inside Artifacts
**Question:** How should OSLO display intelligence inside artifacts?

**Recommendation:** Mixed system with Cursor/IDE foundation.

**Includes:**
- IDE-style diagnostics
- inline rewrite suggestions
- margin intelligence cards
- explainability drill-down
- conversational expansion

---

## Q17 — Interruption Aggressiveness
**Question:** How aggressively should OSLO interrupt users?

**Recommendation:** Severity-based mix, assistive by default.

**Levels:**
1. Informational
2. Assistive
3. Assertive
4. Governance Gate

---

## Q18 — Confidence Display
**Question:** Should users see a single confidence score or decomposed confidence?

**Recommendation:** Dynamic layered decomposition.

**Rationale:** Single scores create false precision. Full decomposition creates overload.

---

## Q19 — Live Intelligence Stream
**Question:** Should users see a live stream of OSLO thinking and observations?

**Recommendation:** Expandable event timeline with small persistent signal surface.

**Rationale:** Avoid turning the UI into telemetry.

---

## Q20 — Multiple Simultaneous Views
**Question:** Should OSLO support multiple simultaneous views?

**Recommendation:** Yes, split-pane and multi-context workspaces.

**Rationale:** Outcome reasoning is inherently cross-referential.
