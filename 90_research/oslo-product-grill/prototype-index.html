Slice 10 — Tiering & Limits D134–D142

The cumulative build — Slices 1 through 10 in one file, 13,237 lines, with DL-102 · DL-103 · DL-104 folded in. Live Free→Basic tier in Alpha. One honest limit — analyses per month, never tokens (rendered pending re-derivation; nothing enforced). Judgment quality is never tiered. The upgrade-prompt engine, with the limit-reached rule at every cap — controls stay enabled, the attempt is gated, and every prompt names which limit you hit. The new Reports surface (reliability-qualified; it packages understanding, it never produces it). And the in-product tier-definitions census, which shows the user exactly what nobody has decided yet.
Open the final prototype →
The retired levers are shown as struck, out loud. The priority queue / "Basic runs it now" lever is named on the Plans page as the upgrade we deliberately did not build — an async product cannot sell speed. Outcome-based pricing is named as prohibited. UP-1 (daily fixes) · UP-2 (daily chat) · UP-5 (deep runs/day) are retired: chat is now uncapped on every tier. 17 runtime boot assertions fail loudly if any of it is quietly undone.
Why this one, and only this one. Refinements were folded forward, never backward. A defect corrected in slice 8 was fixed in slices 8, 9 and 10 — it was not back-ported into slices 1–7. So the earlier files carry known refinement debt (superseded copy, pre-cascade shells, decisions later reversed) and are kept as a record of the sequence, not as a spec. Demo slice 10. Read the earlier slices only to see how a decision landed.
