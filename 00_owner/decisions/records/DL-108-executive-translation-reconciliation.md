# DL-108 — Reconcile design-guidance 'Executive Translation' with the reporting doctrine — tailor the ask, never the read

- **Date:** 2026-07-12 · **Status:** Ratified · **Decided by:** Idris (Founder Console)
- **Class:** A

This decision resolves a conflict between an **incoming non-canonical design-guidance document** — the philosophy *"Designing for Evolving Organizational Understanding"* — and the ratified reporting doctrine. It introduces **no** new object, invariant, or capability, and edits no canonical artifact; it records the owner's decision on how the incoming guidance is to be adopted, and reaffirms existing canon. Routed under the Framework 001 Conflict Resolution Rule ("conflicts are resolved through governance proposals rather than direct edits").

## Decision

Adopt **canon's formulation — "tailor the ASK, never the READ"** — as the governing rule for any "executive translation" / audience-adaptation guidance, and **amend the incoming philosophy document's §4D before it is adopted** as a non-canonical doctrine-orientation reference:

1. The §4D principle "the same event is rendered differently for different audiences" is replaced by the ratified rule: **one honest read, many asks.** Sections §1–§3 of a report (the read, the limiting dimension, the unknowns) are **identical for every audience**; only the "what I need from you" section is addressed to the recipient (DL-107).
2. Audience adaptation is confined to **language and altitude and the ask** — never the substance or confidence of the assessment. OSLO's ratified "two altitudes" (impact *for the plan* vs *for the goal*) is the sanctioned way to vary altitude, and the outcome altitude is a **structural** claim ("does the plan, as written, still reach its stated intent?"), **never a forecast** of success (DL-107).
3. "One Truth, Multiple Lenses" (§11 of the guidance) is retained — it is consistent with canon (Narrative Views are "views of understanding, not static reports") **provided** the lens is language and the ask, not the assessment.
4. Any surface derived from §4D is bound by the **P1 defect classes** (DL-104): a report a reader could mistake for health / RAG / readiness / probability of success is a P1 Health-framing defect; any un-reliability-qualified claim, or derived content dressed as attested, is a P1 Overclaim defect.

The amended philosophy document is adopted as a **non-canonical doctrine-orientation reference** only; `RELEASE_1_REPORTING_SPECIFICATION_V1` remains authoritative on reporting.

## Conditions

1. **Assessment identical across audiences** — no §4D-derived surface re-frames the read (§1–§3) by audience; only the ask/decisions section is recipient-addressed.
2. **Language may vary; claims may not** — audience adaptation is wording and altitude, never the substance or confidence of the assessment.
3. **No forecast** — outcome-altitude statements are structural, never predictions of success.
4. **P1 guard** — any surface mistakable for health/RAG/readiness/probability, or any derived-as-attested claim, is a P1 defect (DL-104) and blocks.
5. **Reference-only** — the amended philosophy document is a non-canonical orientation reference, not a spec; the ratified Reporting Specification remains authoritative.

## Supersedes / Amends

Amends the **incoming non-canonical philosophy document** (§4D wording + examples) as a precondition of its adoption. **Reaffirms** DL-107 (Tailor-the-ask / Obey-don't-narrate) and DL-104 (P1 Health-framing / Overclaim defect classes). **Supersedes no ratified decision.** No canonical artifact is edited by this decision.
