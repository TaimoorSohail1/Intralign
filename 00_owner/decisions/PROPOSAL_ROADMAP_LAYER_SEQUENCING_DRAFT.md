## Context

OSLO's defensible position is the **governance/orchestration layer above human and AI execution systems** (Positioning §2, §10). Analysis of replication/commoditization risk concluded that the durable moat is **altitude + cross-system neutrality + accumulated outcome corpus**, not the cognitive behaviors themselves (which frontier models increasingly absorb). The strategic exposure is the **entry wedge** ("60-second planning intelligence", §4), which is capability-shaped and therefore the most absorbable — and the most likely to **collapse into a feature of an incumbent execution tool** unless OSLO reaches the layer position first.

This proposal captures the **sequencing rule** that governs how the §7 Strategic Objective Ladder is realized after R1: establish cross-platform breadth at the understanding/governance altitude **before** descending into execution-phase governance. It sequences **existing** ladder levels; it does not add capabilities or doctrine.

## Proposed principle (the decision content)

> **Layer-Before-Depth.** OSLO establishes cross-platform planning-governance **breadth** at the understanding/governance altitude before descending into execution-phase governance **depth**. Altitude (being *above* multiple systems) and neutrality — not phase coverage — constitute the "layer."

Realization order:

1. **Prove planning-governance value** — R1, §7 levels 1–2. **Unchanged**; R1 stays narrow (CHG-064, DL-076). May be single-context.
2. **Establish cross-platform neutrality** — planning-intelligence governance supports **≥2 meaningful execution/planning platforms** (e.g., Jira · Asana · MS Project) as governed sources, with real per-platform depth. This is what *constitutes* the layer (altitude + neutrality) and is the anti-collapse moat incumbents are structurally disqualified from holding.
3. **Then descend into execution intelligence / execution governance** — §7 levels 4–5 — governing execution that occurs *in those external platforms*, from the established altitude, carrying the accumulated interpretation-risk corpus (§8).
4. **Execution-phase read-only visibility** (outcome observation) is permitted **earlier** as a feedback input, **distinct from** execution governance.
5. **Advisory-only invariant (DL-047) preserved throughout** — "execution governance" means governing/observing execution in external systems; OSLO never executes or coordinates work itself.

## Findings

1. **Traces cleanly up to canon.** The principle sequences the §7 Strategic Objective Ladder and the DL-034 Evolution Framework **Execution Depth** axis (Doctrine 10) without redefining either. It is consistent with Positioning §2/§10 (layer above execution) and §9 non-goals (not a work-coordination tool).
2. **Same genre as DL-076.** A non-doctrinal product-scope/roadmap orientation decision that reconciles/sequences existing axes — mirrors the DL-076 (release ladder) and DL-075 (lifecycle crosswalk) pattern.
3. **Addresses a real strategic risk** (wedge-collapse) with a sequencing rule rather than a new capability — lowest-cost, lowest-drift intervention.
4. **R1-safe.** Applies to R2+; explicitly does not alter R1 scope.

## Concerns

1. **Doctrine-vs-product boundary.** The principle *relies on* the Execution Depth axis (Doctrine 10) and the advisory-only invariant (DL-047), both doctrinal. It does **not** change them, so it is dispositioned as product-roadmap canon per owner selection. **Anti-Assumption flag:** if the owner later wants the sequencing itself to carry doctrinal force, it must be re-routed as a doctrine change — not assumed here.
2. **"≥2 platforms" is a neutrality threshold, not an integration commitment.** Specific platform targets and timing remain product-scope authorship (RELEASE_MODEL_AND_ALPHA_LADDER, OSLO_LINEAR_INITIATIVES), not fixed by this decision.
3. **Conditional moat.** Durability assumes (a) the structure yields outcomes the bare model can't and (b) buyers value an external, auditable governance layer enough to pay for it rather than self-serve via a frontier model. This is recorded as a monitored assumption, not a guarantee.
